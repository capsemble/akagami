# Biology Notes — Protein Synthesis (Gene Expression)

## 1. The central idea

The information in a gene is stored in DNA, but DNA never leaves the nucleus in a eukaryotic cell. A protein is built from that information in two main stages: transcription, which copies the gene into messenger RNA (mRNA) inside the nucleus, and translation, which reads the mRNA at a ribosome in the cytoplasm to join amino acids into a polypeptide.

Common misconception: "DNA is translated directly into protein." It is not. The ribosome only ever reads mRNA.

## 2. Transcription (in the nucleus)

- RNA polymerase binds to the promoter, a region of DNA just before the gene.
- The enzyme unwinds a short stretch of the double helix and uses one strand, the template strand, to build a complementary RNA strand.
- RNA uses uracil (U) where DNA uses thymine (T).
- When RNA polymerase reaches the terminator sequence, it releases the new RNA molecule. This first copy is called pre-mRNA.

## 3. RNA processing (still in the nucleus)

Pre-mRNA cannot be translated as it is. Before it leaves the nucleus:

- A 5′ cap (a modified guanine) is added to the front end. The cap protects the RNA and is what the ribosome later recognises.
- A poly-A tail of many adenine nucleotides is added to the 3′ end, which protects the molecule from being broken down.
- Splicing removes the introns (non-coding sections) and joins the exons (coding sections) together.

Common misconception: "Introns are translated and then cut out of the protein." In fact introns are removed from the RNA before translation ever begins.

The result is mature mRNA, which leaves the nucleus through a nuclear pore.

## 4. Translation (in the cytoplasm, at a ribosome)

- **Initiation:** the small ribosomal subunit binds the mRNA at the 5′ cap and scans along it until it finds the start codon, AUG. A tRNA carrying methionine pairs with AUG, and the large subunit joins to complete the ribosome.
- **Elongation:** each codon (three bases) on the mRNA is matched by a tRNA whose anticodon is complementary to it. Each tRNA carries one specific amino acid. The ribosome forms a peptide bond between the growing chain and the new amino acid, then moves along by one codon.
- **Termination:** when the ribosome reaches a stop codon (UAA, UAG or UGA), no tRNA matches it. A release factor binds instead, and the finished polypeptide is released.

Common misconception: "tRNA carries the codons." tRNA carries an anticodon and an amino acid; the codons are on the mRNA.

## 5. After translation

The polypeptide folds into its three-dimensional shape, sometimes helped by chaperone proteins. Only the folded protein can do its job. A change in the DNA sequence can change a codon, which can change an amino acid, which can change how the protein folds.

## 6. Summary of the order

The gene is transcribed into pre-mRNA, the pre-mRNA is capped, tailed and spliced, the mature mRNA is exported to the cytoplasm, the ribosome finds the start codon, tRNAs add amino acids codon by codon, a stop codon ends translation, and the polypeptide folds into a working protein. Each step needs the product of the step before it.
