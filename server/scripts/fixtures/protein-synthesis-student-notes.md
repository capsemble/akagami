Protein Synthesis: From DNA to Protein
Protein synthesis is the process by which cells use genetic information to produce proteins.
It involves transcription and translation, with RNA processing occurring in eukaryotic cells.
1. DNA Stores Genetic Information
DNA contains genetic instructions encoded in the sequence of its nucleotide bases.
A gene is a segment of DNA that can provide instructions for producing a functional product, such as a protein.
2. Transcription
During transcription, RNA polymerase uses a DNA template to synthesize a complementary RNA molecule.
In eukaryotic cells, transcription of protein-coding genes occurs in the nucleus.
The initial RNA transcript contains information derived from the DNA template.
3. RNA Processing
In eukaryotes, the initial pre-mRNA undergoes processing before it is exported from the nucleus.
Important processing steps include:
- Addition of a 5′ cap.
- Addition of a poly-A tail.
- RNA splicing.
During splicing, introns are removed and exons are joined together.
The resulting mature mRNA can then leave the nucleus and enter the cytoplasm.
Important: Introns are not inherently harmful or a form of biological sabotage. They are normal components of many eukaryotic genes and are removed from pre-mRNA during conventional splicing.
4. Translation
Translation occurs at ribosomes.
The ribosome reads the codons of the mRNA. Transfer RNA molecules help deliver amino acids corresponding to those codons.
The amino acids are joined to form a growing polypeptide chain.
Translation generally begins at a start codon and proceeds until a stop codon is reached.
5. Protein Formation
The newly synthesized polypeptide folds and may undergo additional processing or modification.
Its final structure and function depend on its amino acid sequence and subsequent cellular processes.
6. How the Stages Depend on One Another
For a typical protein-coding gene in a eukaryotic cell:
1. DNA provides the genetic template.
2. Transcription produces pre-mRNA.
3. RNA processing produces mature mRNA.
4. Mature mRNA is exported to the cytoplasm.
5. Ribosomes translate the mRNA into a polypeptide.
6. The polypeptide folds and may undergo additional processing.
These steps describe a simplified pathway. Actual gene expression involves additional regulation and complexity.
