# The Old Library — scrolls the Oracle reads from

Each scroll begins with a heading line `## <id> — <title>`. The text under it is
what retrieval cuts into chunks. Chunks never cross from one scroll into the
next, exactly as a real index keeps documents apart.

## s12 — The scriptorium ledger, ninth volume
Candles are counted at dawn and again at dusk. The east shelf was re-oiled and the ladder rail mended. A delivery of vellum arrived short by six sheets, returned. On the third night the Archivist recorded a dose of forty milligrams, taken at dusk, after the blue flame had settled. The flame was lit at the seventh bell and the ledger closed at the eighth. No hand but the Archivist's appears on the page.

## s19 — Letters on the old standard
The old standard was sixty milligrams at dawn, though that practice was abandoned two winters ago. The Archivist disputed the sixty-milligram standard in a letter never sent. Copies of the old rule still hang in the lower reading room, yellowed and unrevised.

## s04 — Customs of the southern chapter house
Doses in the southern chapter house are recorded in grains, not milligrams. Their scribes write the hour by the bell rather than by the light, and they keep their ledgers in red ink.

## s27 — Rules for younger readers
A dose is to be halved for any reader under the age of sixteen. Younger readers may not climb the rolling ladder after dusk, and must return every scroll to the desk by the eighth bell.

## s11 — On the hours of the house
Dusk is reckoned from the moment the west stair falls into shadow. Dawn is reckoned from the first bell. The night porter keeps the hours when the bells are silent.

## s02 — Notes on the blue flame
Blue flame settles roughly a quarter hour after it is lit. A flame that will not settle is a draught under the door, and the porter is sent to find it.

## s31 — The apprentice's survey
The apprentice measured forty paces from the east door to the reading desk, and sixty from the desk to the bindery. The survey was drawn on the back of a spoiled page.

## s07 — Accounts of the autumn repairs
A full accounting of the autumn repairs, the stair, the lamp oil, the bindery debts, and the names of every apprentice who signed the roll that season. The roof over the stacks was patched twice and still leaks in a west wind.

## s05 — Of the Binder and his patron
The Binder re-cut the scrolls of the east shelf at the request of a patron, and approved the new bindings himself. He signs his work with a small knot drawn in the margin.
